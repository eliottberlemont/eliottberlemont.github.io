Project Revenant

Project Manager · UMBRA · Fall 2026 – present

Project Revenant is a closed-loop controls project, focused on keeping the vehicle vertical by actively moving exterior control surfaces. Using real-time data processing and control algorithms, Revenant's on-board avionics will stabilize the flight through servo-actuated canards driving pitch, yaw, and roll. The canards are the stabilization system, not trim surfaces added to an already-stable rocket — the fins are sized as a passive floor and nothing more.

I currently lead a large team comprised of over 20 different engineers across five different subteams: Avionics, Aerodynamics, Mechanisms & Actuations, Systems, and Manufacturing. I manage technical scope, budgeting, scheduling, and outreach.

Where the project is now

Revenant is in its first design phase. My role is to oversee different conceptual designs and control philosophies, guiding team members in the direction that best fits the goal of the project. Using software such as OpenRocket, we are developing models with placeholder materials while we flesh out our budget and bill of materials. Working with the other subteams, we are also narrowing down real densities of dynamic components and avionics systems. Through simulation we have successfully developed a non-controlled rocket that remains stable during flight. This will allow us to launch a scale version of the launch vehicle to verify core data points such as apogee, velocity, and acceleration. Our goal is to fully finish designing a true-scale rocket with active controls by the end of the 2027 spring semester.

Project Inferno — Liquid Engine Test DAQ

Simulation & Software Engineer · UMBRA Electronics · Spring 2026 – present

Project INFERNO is a completely student-designed liquid engine test stand: a dual-propellant, pressure-fed system with cryogenic and variable-flow capabilities. It serves as a reusable test bed for liquid engine research, including but not limited to regenerative cooling, new propellant combinations, thrust vectoring, and throttling. This year the team is assembling its first iteration around the in-house FREEDOM engine design.

My contribution

On Project INFERNO, I worked on data acquisition, specifically valve control. Using a four-layer board built around an STM32H7 processor and a Raspberry Pi Compute Module, I troubleshot connection issues, GUI systems, valve actuation, and sensor sampling speeds.

Project Exo Bronco — Two-Stage Flight Avionics

Avionics Systems Liaison · UMBRA Electronics · Spring 2026 – present

Exo Bronco is a two-stage high-powered collegiate rocket. Both stages fly using custom avionics PCBs, running Python flight code and carrying several different sensors. These computers record data to ensure that the stage separation on the rocket falls within safe and standard operating procedures, and are critical to the success of the mission.

My contribution

My role on Exo Bronco's avionics subteam was troubleshooting and further developing custom Python flight code. My work mainly focused on sensors such as the BNO055 and ADXL375 — specifically how fast they sampled data at different points during flight, to optimize storage space on the onboard microSD card and packet size for live telemetry.

Platform
CircuitPython on a custom ESP32-S3 based flight board
Sensing
ADXL375 high-G accelerometer, BNO055 9-DOF IMU, MS8607 barometric sensor, GPS
Telemetry
RFM9x LoRa, per-stage frequency and spreading factor plan
Recovery
Four pyro channels with continuity checking, altitude-triggered main deployment
